# UN CCW Group of Governmental Experts on Emerging Technologies in the Area of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS)

- **Stage**: CCW GGE Report & Rolling Text Evolution (`rolling_text_report_2023`)
- **Document Symbol**: `CCW/GGE.1/2023/2`
- **Date**: `2023-05-26`
- **Institutional Author**: `UN CCW GGE <ccw@un.org>`
- **Source**: https://undocs.org/en/CCW/GGE.1/2023/2

> Consensus 2023 Report of the GGE on LAWS establishing two-tier operationalization: prohibition of weapons systems that cannot be used in compliance with IHL or produce uncontrollable effects, and strict regulation (context, duration, geographic scope, human-machine interaction) of other autonomous weapons systems.

## Preamble & Recitals

(1) The Group of Governmental Experts on Emerging Technologies in the Area of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems adopted its consensus 2023 report (CCW/GGE.1/2023/2) on 26 May 2023 in Geneva, advancing the normative and operational framework toward a two-tier structure of prohibitions and regulations.

## Enacting Terms (Articles)

### Article 1 — Applicability of IHL and State Responsibility (Paragraphs 17–19)

1. International law, in particular the United Nations Charter and International Humanitarian Law (IHL), applies fully to all weapons systems, including those based on emerging technologies in the area of lethal autonomous weapons systems (LAWS).
2. Complying with IHL rules and principles, including inter alia distinction, proportionality and precautions in attack, requires inter alia context-specific human judgment and control.
3. Every internationally wrongful act of a State, including one resulting from the use of a weapon system based on emerging technologies in the area of LAWS, entails the international responsibility of that State.

### Article 2 — First Tier — Prohibited Autonomous Weapons Systems (Paragraph 21)

1. Weapons systems based on emerging technologies in the area of lethal autonomous weapons systems that cannot be used in compliance with international humanitarian law must not be used, and their development or production should be prohibited.
2. In particular, weapons systems must not be used if they:
(a) are of a nature to cause superfluous injury or unnecessary suffering;
(b) are inherently indiscriminate; or
(c) are incapable of being directed at a specific military objective or have effects that cannot be limited as required by IHL.

### Article 3 — Second Tier — Regulated Autonomous Weapons Systems (Paragraph 22)

1. For other weapons systems based on emerging technologies in the area of LAWS that select and apply force to targets without human intervention after activation, States must implement rigorous regulatory measures to ensure compliance with IHL, including:
(a) limiting the types of targets that the system can engage;
(b) limiting the duration, geographical scope, and scale of operation, including through self-destruct, self-deactivation, or self-neutralization mechanisms;
(c) ensuring clear human-machine interaction, understandability, and predictability of system behavior in the specific operational environment; and
(d) enabling human operators to supervise, intervene, and deactivate the system where appropriate.

### Article 5 — National Legal Weapons Reviews and Lifecycle Testing (Paragraphs 24–26)

1. National legal reviews of new weapons, means or methods of warfare, including those based on emerging technologies in the area of LAWS, are a critical measure for States to ensure compliance with IHL.
2. Reviews should be conducted at multiple stages of the lifecycle, including study, development, acquisition, and whenever significant modifications (such as machine learning retraining) alter the operational capabilities or effects of the weapon system.
