# United States NIST Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0 & GenAI Profile)

- **Stage**: NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 (NIST AI 100-1) (`nist_ai_rmf_1_0`)
- **Document Symbol**: `NIST AI 100-1`
- **Date**: `2023-01-26`
- **Institutional Author**: `National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) <ai-rmf@nist.gov>`
- **Source**: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf

> Release of NIST AI RMF 1.0 defining trustworthiness characteristics (valid/reliable, safe, secure/resilient, accountable/transparent, explainable/interpretable, privacy-enhanced, fair with harmful bias managed) and the four Core Functions: GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE.

## Preamble & Recitals

(1) The NIST Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, NIST AI 100-1) is intended for voluntary use and to improve the ability to incorporate trustworthiness considerations into the design, development, use, and evaluation of AI products, services, and systems.

## Enacting Terms (Articles)

### Article 1 — Core Function 1 — GOVERN (GV)

1. GOVERN 1: Policies, processes, procedures, and practices across the organization related to the mapping, measuring, and managing of AI risks are in place, transparent, and implemented effectively.
(a) GOVERN 1.1: Legal and regulatory requirements involving AI are understood, managed, and documented.
(b) GOVERN 1.2: The characteristics of trustworthy AI (valid and reliable, safe, secure and resilient, accountable and transparent, explainable and interpretable, privacy-enhanced, and fair with harmful bias managed) are integrated into organizational policies.
(c) GOVERN 1.5: Ongoing monitoring and periodic review of the risk management process and its outcomes are planned, organizational roles and responsibilities clearly defined, including determining the authority to decommission an AI system.

### Article 2 — Core Function 2 — MAP (MP)

1. MAP 1: Context is established and understood across the AI lifecycle.
(a) MAP 1.1: Intended purposes, potentially beneficial uses, context-specific laws, norms and expectations, and prospective settings in which the AI system will be deployed are understood and documented.
(b) MAP 2.3: Scientific integrity and TEVV (Testing, Evaluation, Verification, and Validation) considerations are identified and documented, including those related to experimental design, data collection, and model training.
(c) MAP 5.1: Likelihood and magnitude of each identified impact (both potentially beneficial and harmful) based on expected use, past uses of similar systems, and reasonably foreseeable misuse are determined.

### Article 3 — Core Function 3 — MEASURE (MS)

1. MEASURE 1: Appropriate methods and metrics are identified and applied.
(a) MEASURE 2.5: The AI system to be deployed is demonstrated to be valid and reliable.
(b) MEASURE 2.6: The AI system is evaluated regularly for safety risks, and its safe residual risk threshold is shown to be met.
(c) MEASURE 2.7: AI system security and resilience to adversarial attacks, data poisoning, and model extraction are evaluated and documented.
(d) MEASURE 2.11: Fairness and bias — including systemic, computational/statistical, and human-cognitive biases — are evaluated and results documented.

### Article 4 — Core Function 4 — MANAGE (MG)

1. MANAGE 1: AI risks based on assessments and other analytical output from the MAP and MEASURE functions are prioritized, responded to, and managed.
(a) MANAGE 2.4: Mechanisms are in place and applied to sustain the value of deployed AI systems, and to supersede, disengage, or deactivate AI systems that demonstrate performance or outcomes inconsistent with intended use.
(b) MANAGE 3.1: AI risks and benefits from third-party resources and value chain components are regularly monitored, and risk controls are applied and documented.
(c) MANAGE 4.1: Post-deployment AI system monitoring plans are implemented, including mechanisms for capturing user feedback, appeal and override workflows, and incident response.
