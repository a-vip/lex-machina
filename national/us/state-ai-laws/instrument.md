> Colorado's high-risk algorithmic discrimination statute (SB 24-205, subsequently refined via SB 26-189 following xAI v. Weiser) with NIST AI RMF / ISO 42001 safe harbor, plus California AB 2013 training-data transparency and SB 53 (TFAIA) frontier safety disclosures.

## Enacting Terms (Articles)

### Article 1 — Colorado Artificial Intelligence Act (SB 24-205 -> Revised by SB 26-189)

Structured statutory record (enacted May 17, 2024; later amended/replaced via SB 26-189 following xAI v. Weiser): Established a reasonable-care duty for developers and deployers of high-risk AI / automated decision-making technology (ADMT) in consequential decisions (employment, education, lending, housing, healthcare, insurance, legal services) to prevent algorithmic discrimination, providing a rebuttable presumption of reasonable care when aligned with NIST AI RMF or ISO/IEC 42001.

### Article 2 — California AB 2013 & SB 53 (Transparency in Frontier AI Act — TFAIA)

Structured statutory record (2024-2026): AB 2013 (effective Jan 1, 2026) mandates high-level generative AI training dataset transparency disclosures; SB 53 (TFAIA) targets frontier foundation models exceeding 10^26 FLOPs with public catastrophic risk frameworks, critical safety incident reporting to California OES, and frontier lab whistleblower protections.
